Finding, indexing and transcribing every crossword ever printed

The first crossword appeared in 1913. Since then countless newspapers, magazines and books have printed them, and most survive only on paper, or as page scans you can look at but not search. We catalog the publications, index every puzzle they ran, and transcribe each one into an xd file, linked to the scan it was read from, so that it can be searched, studied and preserved. The finished puzzles join the xd Crossword Corpus, a research archive of crosswords from 1913 to today.

This is a research archive, not a place to play old puzzles. Inspired by David Steinberg's Pre-Shortzian Puzzle Project, whose volunteers digitized the New York Times crosswords of the pre-digital era.

Ways to help

You've seen a crossword somewhere

Tell us the publication and the date: report a puzzle. Know a paper, magazine, book or site that ran them? Report a publication. A paper copy in front of you counts.

You have a Claude account

Direct your Claude to review puzzles: it checks each OCR draft against the scan, square by square and clue by clue, and sends its corrections as a pull request in your name. How to help has the prompt to paste. Or point it at an open lead in the catalog and ask it to find where that publication's pages survive.

You have Newspapers.com or a library card

Each status page lists the pages it still wants: a missing Sunday, a cleaner scan, an answer key. Find one, clip it, and open an issue with the date and the clipping link. Please don't upload page images; issues are public.

You have an eye for detail

Check transcriptions yourself on the review site: each clue is shown beside its scan. No account needed.
